The overview most banking, budgeting and treasury apps open on: a total, one card per account and the latest activity. Everything that happens next keeps your eye on the money.

## Things to try

- **Open an account.** Its card grows into the page header, the other accounts shrink into a row of switches, and the list becomes that account's transactions.
- **Switch accounts** from that row. Shops both accounts were used at (Corner Coffee, City Transit) stay where they are; only their amounts change.
- **Move money.** The button opens into the transfer form. Pick From, To and an amount, then confirm: the amount leaves one card, lands in the other, and both balances count to their new values. The new rows say "Pending", then settle.
- **Try to overdraw.** Amounts larger than the balance are disabled, and the button says why.
- **Press Escape** to close the form, or go back to all accounts.

## How it works

```tsx
<Remold.Layout trigger={[open, panel, flight?.stage]}>
  {open ? <AccountPage account={open} /> : <Overview accounts={accounts} />}
  <Activity rows={rows} />
</Remold.Layout>
```

The overview and the account page are different markup in different parents, with nothing keyed across them. `Layout` matches each card, balance and row by what it shows, so the card you clicked becomes the header. The amount in a transfer is one small chip rendered inside the sending card, then inside the receiving one; `Layout` sees the same chip in a new place and moves it there. `Text` changes only the digits that change.

The numbers add up: the total is the sum of the accounts, and a transfer moves exactly what it says.
