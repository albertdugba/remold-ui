Tap the orb to translate your own voice between Spanish and English.

## Things to try

- **Tap the orb and speak.** It goes through each state in turn: *listening* (the microphone is open and the orb waits), *hearing you* (it moves like a voice as your words appear), *translating* (the clouds gather while the last words are finished) and *speaking* (the translation is read aloud). Tap again to finish early or stop it talking.
- **Watch the translation correct itself.** Mid-sentence, "la estación" can read as *season*; once the sentence ends it becomes *train station*. `Remold.Text` changes only the words that changed, so the line settles instead of flickering.
- **Swap languages** with the arrows between them.

Live mode uses [react-speech-recognition](https://www.npmjs.com/package/react-speech-recognition) for captions, the on-device [Translator API](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/translator-api) in recent Chrome, and the browser's speech synthesis. Where translation isn't available it still captions what you said, and says so.

## The orb on its own

```tsx
// Feed a 0–1 level from your own audio or agent:
<VoiceOrb state="speaking" level={amplitude} />
```

Without a `level`, it simulates a believable voice while listening or speaking. `state` is `idle | listening | thinking | speaking`; `theme` is `aurora | ember | mono`; `size` sets the square size.

## How it works

- The sphere is a clipped circle filled with a sky gradient, a few soft cloud blobs drifting on their own orbits, a deep current along the bottom, a highlight on the top left and a gentle shade at the edge. No outline, no rings.
- It's a [`Remold.Canvas`](/docs/primitives/canvas). Each state is a set of `values` (size, drift speed, breathing, how tightly the clouds gather), so the orb springs from wherever it is into the next state. The canvas handles sizing, the frame loop and reduced motion; the component is the paint function.
- The voice level, from your `level` prop or the simulation, rises fast and falls slowly, so the orb leaps to a word and eases back. It also speeds the clouds up.
- It's `continuous`, so it keeps drifting between state changes. It pauses off screen, and reduced motion holds it still.

<Callout>
  The orb never touches the microphone. Live translation hands your voice to the browser's speech recognition, which in Chrome is processed by Google's servers; translation itself runs on the device. The component sends nothing anywhere itself.
</Callout>
