## Things to try

- **Tap the orb and speak.** It goes through each state in turn, and the line under it names each one: *connecting* (the ball unwinds into one arc chasing its tail while the microphone and translator start), *listening for Spanish* (the arc opens into rings and waits), *hearing you* (a ripple as tall as your voice runs down the rings as your words appear), *translating* (the rings gather into three balls that lift one after another, like someone typing, while the last words are finished) and *speaking* (the dots crowd the rim of a ball, which ripples and frays with the voice as the translation is read aloud). Tap again to finish early or stop it talking.
- **Watch the translation correct itself.** Mid-sentence, "la estación" can read as *season*; once the sentence ends it becomes *train station*. `Remold.Text` changes only the words that changed, so the line settles instead of flickering.
- **Read the line under the orb.** It says what is happening in plain words, with the language in it: *Listening for Spanish*, *Translating to English*, *Speaking English*. Whenever something is going on the words shimmer: they sit back in grey while a band of light sweeps along them. At rest, *Tap to speak* holds still.
- **Swap languages** with the arrows between them.

Live mode uses [react-speech-recognition](https://www.npmjs.com/package/react-speech-recognition) for captions, the on-device [Translator API](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/translator-api) in recent Chrome, and the browser's speech synthesis. Where translation isn't available it still captions what you said, and says so.

## The orb on its own

```tsx
// Feed a 0–1 level from your own audio or agent:
<VoiceOrb state="speaking" level={amplitude} />

// Say what it's doing under it, in its own words or yours:
<VoiceOrb state="connecting" status />
<VoiceOrb state="searching" status="Searching the web" />
```

Without a `level`, it makes up a believable voice while listening or speaking. `state` is `idle | connecting | listening | thinking | searching | speaking`; `theme` is `aurora | ember | mono`; `size` sets the square size.

`status` adds a line of text under the orb, so nobody has to learn what each shape means. `status` alone uses the state's own word: *Ready*, *Connecting*, *Listening*, *Thinking*, *Searching* or *Speaking*. Better, pass what your agent is really doing, such as *Searching the web* or *Reading 6 sources*: the glass keeps looking about and only the line changes. It sits in a `role="status"` element, so screen readers hear each change.

## How it works

- **Every state is a shape.** Each one is built by chaining Remold's [`shapes`](/docs/primitives/particles#3d-shapes): the idle ball is `shapes.sphere()`, the listening rings are tori stacked with `shapes.compose`, the three thinking dots are three small spheres, and the glass is a torus for the rim with a curve each for the handle and the glint. `scale`, `rotate`, `at`, `warp` and `fuzz` do the moving: the ball breathes with `scale`, the arc chases its tail with `rotate`, and the speaking edge is a ring pushed in and out with `warp` and shaken loose with `fuzz`.
- **It's a [`Remold.Particles`](/docs/primitives/particles).** Ten times a second the orb builds the shape for that moment and hands it over. A shape gives the same points in the same order every time, so with `pairing="index"` each dot keeps its place from one moment to the next and springs to it. The motion stays smooth, and a state change can interrupt another halfway.
- **The status line is a [`Remold.Text`](/docs/primitives/text).** *Connecting*, *Listening*, *Thinking* and *Speaking* share most of their letters, so the line changes with the orb instead of blinking. In every state but idle it shimmers: a CSS mask dims the words and sweeps a band of full colour along them. The mask sits on the line, not the letters, so it keeps sweeping while they morph. Reduced motion shows the plain words.
- **Shapes you already know.** Thinking is the three dots of someone typing and searching is a magnifying glass, so neither needs explaining. Both face you while the ball and the rings turn.
- **The voice level** comes from your `level` prop or a made-up one. It rises fast and falls slowly, so the orb leaps to a word and eases back.
- **Light and dark.** On a dark page the dots glow by adding their light together; on a light page they are drawn in deeper colours instead.
- It pauses off screen, and reduced motion holds it still.

<Callout>
  The orb never touches the microphone. Live translation hands your voice to the browser's speech recognition, which in Chrome is processed by Google's servers; translation itself runs on the device. The component sends nothing anywhere itself.
</Callout>
