It walks through the five steps by itself while it's on screen. Click a step, or touch it, and it's yours.

## Things to try

- **Upload.** The laptop's screen fills like a progress bar as the file arcs into the bucket on the cloud, and the bucket's band rises.
- **Optimise.** Three smaller sizes split off the original and stand on the resize function's chip, one after another, while the numbers along the top change from 4.2 MB to 180 KB.
- **Distribute.** A copy flies out to each edge server in turn, and each edge's sides fill as its copy lands.
- **Serve.** The edges light up and requests flow on to the phones beside them. The label over a phone turns into its response time.
- **Update, then Upload again.** The new version waits on the app, the old sizes fade to outlines and the edges drop their copies, so the next upload starts the story over.
- **Jump between any two steps.** Everything springs from wherever it is, including halfway through an arc.
- **Switch the provider** in the panel below. The labels and captions change to Google Cloud's or Cloudflare's names, or plain ones.

## Props

<ControlsTable name="edge-delivery" />

`provider` picks ready-made names (`aws`, `gcp`, `cloudflare`, `generic`), and `names` overrides any of them: `names={{ bucket: "Media bucket", region: "us-east-1" }}`. The scene draws no provider's logos or artwork, only these words. The steps, their captions and their numbers are in `STAGES`; the edge servers are in `EDGES`. Each step is one scene layout built in `buildScene`, so changing the story means changing where things sit in each step.

## How it works

- Every object (the laptop and its screen, the cloud, the bucket, the resize chip, each edge, each phone, the file, its three sizes and each edge's copy) exists in every step. A step only says where each one sits and how it looks, and [Scene](/docs/primitives/scene) springs between them.
- Moves use `arc` to hop instead of slide and `delay` to go one after another. Copies that don't exist yet wait inside the original with `opacity: 0`, so they appear to split off it.
- The bucket is an `ellipse` with a deep `well`, so the file and its sizes sit inside it, and its `fill` rises as it stores more. The laptop's screen is a thin box standing on the base, whose `fill` is the upload's progress bar. The cloud is the usual cloud outline turned to face the viewer (`faceViewer`). Edges and phones fill with the accent as they start serving.
- The labels are HTML laid over the canvas and placed after every frame from `anchor(id, "back")`. The numbers and the caption are [Text](/docs/primitives/text), so only the characters that change move.
