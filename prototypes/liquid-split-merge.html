Liquid split and merge

One shape becomes two, or two become one, the way motion designers animate it: a liquid neck stretches and snaps, and the shapes squash and wobble with their own speed. Tap a stage, or tap again mid-motion to reverse it.

Delete becomes a confirmation

A destructive action asks for confirmation by splitting in two, so the choice appears exactly where the finger already is.

Filter chips merge into a tag

Three chips flow together into one summary. Merging back shows exactly which filters it holds.

A status pill buds off a control

A compact status grows when recording starts, and a stop button drips off its side.

How it works: each shape is a capsule driven by springs, so it keeps its speed when you interrupt it. Between shapes that belong together, a curved bridge (the metaball technique) is drawn from tangent points on their end caps; it thins as they part and snaps past a distance. Squash and stretch scale each capsule along its velocity while keeping its area. In Remold the split or merge itself comes from the matcher; in this prototype each one is declared by hand.
