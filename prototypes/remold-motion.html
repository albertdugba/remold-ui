Fluid motion for interfacesthat an AI just rewrote.

Remold works out what became what when your UI changes, then moves it the way iOS does: on springs, with your finger's speed, and interruptible at any moment. No ids, no keys, no layoutId.

Every demo below swaps its HTML wholesale. Turn on Slow motion to catch a transition halfway, and Show matches to see what was paired (dashed) and what arrived (dotted).

Tool calls that become results

An assistant calls tools, waits, sometimes fails, then shows what came back. Each step is new markup. Remold sees that the route in "Searching flights" is the route on the flight card, so the call grows into its result.

9:41

Try this

  • Press Play. Both tools start; the flight comes back first and the forecast follows.
  • Turn on Flight search fails once and play again. The call turns red, says why, then retries and still grows into its result.
  • Press Price drop, then Book in the phone. The old fare slides aside, and the card becomes a confirmation without losing its place.
  • Jump between steps quickly. Every card turns around from wherever it is.
<Remold.Root spring="snappy">
  {parts.map((p) => p.state === "result"
    ? <FlightCard {...p.result} />
    : <ToolCall name={p.tool} args={p.args} />)}
</Remold.Root>

Illustrations that are alive

Product pages explain themselves with layered isometric art, then ship it as a flat image. These four scenes are drawn live on canvas from a few lines of data each, so they respond to you and change shape the way the product does.

Try this

  • Switch scenes. The old one sinks away and the new one rises piece by piece.
  • In Database, press Sharded: the single database splits into three shards. Replicated merges them back and grows two copies.
  • In Edge network, compare One region with Edge, then press Live load. The towers become a 3D bar chart of traffic.
  • In Integrations, press Orbit and drag the scene while it spins. Nothing stutters.
  • Hover to lift a piece, click to focus it, drag sideways to turn the scene, and fling it to feel it spring back.
const scene = isoScene(canvas, {
  objects: [
    { name: "primary", look: "cylinder" },
    { name: "replica", look: "cylinder" },
    { name: "api",     look: "chip" },
  ],
  layouts: { single, sharded, replicated },
  links: [["api", "primary"], ["primary", "replica", { dashed: true }]],
});
scene.to("sharded"); // pieces, camera and links all spring

Not just diagrams

The same springs and the same idea of "what becomes what" work for any picture: a globe that unrolls into a map, cards you can flick, cubes that rebuild into new shapes, a screen that comes apart into its layers.

Try this

  • Globe: fling it to spin, then press Flat map. About 2,000 dots unroll to their own spots on the map, and the routes bend with them.
  • Wallet: point at a card and it tilts toward you with a moving glare. Click to flip it. In Cover flow, flick sideways and your speed picks the card that lands in the middle.
  • Voxels: press through the shapes. Each cube flies to the nearest free spot, spare ones shrink away, and the stack builds from the bottom. Sweep your pointer across to push cubes up.
  • Layers: press Exploded and point at a layer to lift it out and see what it is.

Apple's rules, as defaults

      
Each rule from Designing Fluid Interfaces maps to a piece of the library, and to the lesson where you build it.

    

    

      
RuleWhat Remold doesWhere it lives

      
        
Springs set by feelspring({ duration, bounce }), with smooth, snappy and bouncy presets shaped like SwiftUI's.core/spring, lesson 5b

        
Always interruptibleA new change starts from where everything is on screen right now, never from the last resting place.dom/transition, lesson 9

        
Speed carries overA gesture's release speed, or an interrupted animation's speed, becomes the next spring's starting speed.core/spring, lesson 5b

        
Momentum projectionproject(velocity) estimates where a flick would coast to, so the right detent wins.core/gesture

        
Rubber-bandingResistance past an edge with Apple's 0.55 constant, instead of a hard stop.core/gesture

        
Grows from what you tappedThe element you tapped is matched to its destination, so motion starts there and returns there.core/match, lessons 7–8

        
Independent axesx, y, width, height and corner radius each get their own spring.dom/transition

        
Instant responsePress feedback on pointer-down; a drag starts only after 10 px, so taps stay taps.dom/press

        
Reduced motionElements jump into place and content crossfades, so what changed is still visible.dom/transition
