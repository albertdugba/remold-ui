# Lesson 2.2 · A spring (script for Claude)

**Files:** `packages/core/src/motion/spring.ts` (`stepSpring`, `isSettled`) and `motion.test.ts`.
**Goal:** Albert can explain the two forces in a spring, why we take small fixed steps, and why the velocity is updated before the position.
**Mode:** fast, as in 2.1.

## A · Story
A ball on a rubber band tied to a target. The band pulls harder the further away the ball is (**stiffness**). The air slows the ball the faster it goes (**damping**). Each tiny step: work out the push, change the speed, then move.

## B · Read `stepSpring`
- `acceleration = -stiffness × distance − damping × velocity`. Ask him which part is the rubber band and which is the air.
- Velocity first, then position (**semi-implicit Euler**). **Break it:** swap the two lines, then run "moves the same at 30, 60 and 120 fps" and the bouncy test. What happens? *(It gains energy and wobbles more, especially with big steps.)*
- `MAX_STEP = 1/240`: a 1/60 s frame becomes 4 small steps, so 30 fps and 120 fps give the same motion. That's the frame-rate test.

## C · `isSettled`
"Done" means both close *and* slow. Ask: why both? *(A ball passing through the target at full speed is close, but definitely not done.)* This is **perceptual completion**: stop once nobody could see any more movement.
