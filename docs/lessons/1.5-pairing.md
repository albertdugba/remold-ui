# Lesson 1.5 · Which piece goes with which (script for Claude)

**Files:** `packages/core/src/path/measure.ts` (`signedArea`, `centroid`, `bounds`, `samplePoints`), `packages/core/src/morph/match.ts` (`matchShapes`, `reverseSubpath`), with tests beside each.
**Goal:** Albert can explain the four alignment steps and what goes wrong without each one.
**Mode:** fast. The code is written and passing, so teach by story → predict → read → break it → run. One step per message, then wait. Use the playground's "Show points" toggle (`pnpm --filter playground dev`) to *see* each step.
**Code standard:** production open source. No lesson references in `packages/`.

---

## A · Story
Two drawings, A and B. Before anything can slide, every dot in A needs exactly one partner dot in B, and the partners must make sense: the left eye goes to the left eye, and the points travel round the same way, starting from nearby corners. If you get any of that wrong, the shape turns inside out, spins or crumples mid-morph.

## B · Step 1: pairing subpaths
- Read `pairSubpaths`: cost = centre distance + ½ × size difference, cheapest first, nobody shared.
- Predict: A has squares at left and right, and B has the same squares drawn right-first. What pairs with A's left square? *(B's left square. Order doesn't matter in `"auto"`.)*
- Point out: this is **greedy**, not optimal. Part 3 teaches the Hungarian algorithm, which finds the best *total* pairing. Ask him for a case where greedy loses. *(The best first pick steals the partner that a second piece needed much more.)*
- Unpaired pieces: `"collapse"` grows from a point at the piece's own centre; `"merge"` shrinks onto the nearest point of the other outline (good for strokes). Try "Two dots" → "Circle" in the playground.

## C · Step 2: direction (`signedArea`)
- Story: walk round a park. Clockwise or anticlockwise? The shoelace formula's sign tells you. On screen, y points down, so positive means clockwise.
- 🐛 **Bug hunt (Part 1's bug):** copy `match.ts` to a scratch branch, delete the `if (doOrient) sb = orient(sa, sb);` line, and write the issue: *"my heart icon morphs inside out, then fixes itself at the end??"* He reproduces it with an anticlockwise square in the playground (`M50 50 V150 H150 V50 Z`) and finds the line. Confirm only when he's right.

## D · Step 3: equal counts
- Uses `subdivide` from 1.4. Ask: why subdivide *after* orienting, not before? *(It works either way here, but orienting first means the extra points land in the already-final order. That's a good habit for later, when splits get recorded.)*

## E · Step 4: rotation (`bestRotation`)
- Story: two clock faces. Turn B's face until each number sits closest to A's matching number. Try every turn and keep the cheapest (sum of squared distances).
- The `cost < bestCost` early exit in the inner loop: ask why it's safe. *(Costs only grow, so once a turn is worse than the best so far, it can't win.)*
- **Break it:** pass `rotate: false` in the playground code with Square → Star, and watch the points slide sideways.
