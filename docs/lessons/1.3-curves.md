## Part A · What a cubic is

### A1 · Story
A cubic curve is a piece of string pinned at two ends (`p0`, `p3`), with two magnets (`p1`, `p2`) pulling it into a bend. The string never touches the magnets; it just leans towards them. Every smooth shape on screen (letters, icons, circles) is made of these strings laid end to end.

### A2 · Predict (ask, wait)
- If both magnets sit exactly on the straight line between the pins, what shape is the string? *(Straight. That's how a line becomes a cubic.)*

### A3 · Read: `pointAt` in `normalize.ts`
It is three rounds of `lerp` from lesson 1.1: lerp between neighbours, then between those results, then once more. This is **de Casteljau's algorithm**, named after the Citroën engineer who used it to design car bodies.
- Ask: why does `t = 0` always give exactly `p0`? *(Every lerp at t = 0 returns its first point.)*

### A4 · Why only cubics?
Ask him to guess before you explain. *(Morphing pairs piece with piece. If one shape is lines and arcs and the other is quadratics, you can't pair them. If everything is cubics, every piece has the same four points, and morphing is just lerping 8 numbers. Lesson 1.6 depends on this.)*

## Part B · Lines and quadratics

### B1 · `lineToCubic`
Handles at 1/3 and 2/3 along the line, using `lerpPoint`.
- **Break it:** put both handles at 1/2. Predict: does "puts the handles a third…" fail? Does "stays a straight line" fail? *(Only the first. It's still straight, but the speed along it changes. That matters later for morph timing.)*

### B2 · Story: quadratic = one magnet
A quadratic curve has one magnet. To write it as a cubic, give it two magnets, each **two thirds of the way from its pin towards the old magnet**. Same string, exactly.
- Predict `quadToCubic((0,0), (3,3), (6,0))` before showing the test. *(p1 (2,2), p2 (4,2).)*
- **Break it:** use 1/2 instead of 2/3. Which test fails?

## Part C · Lazy curves in the parser: `S` and `T`

### C1 · Story: mirror
`S` says "carry on smoothly". Its first magnet is the last magnet **mirrored through the pen**, like a seesaw: if the last handle was 10 above the pen, the new one is 10 below. Formula: `2 * pen − lastHandle`.
- Predict: `M0 0 C0 10 10 10 10 0 S20 -10 20 0`. Where is the mirrored handle? *(Pen (10, 0), last handle (10, 10), so (10, −10).)*
- Ask: why do `lastC`/`lastQ` get reset after every command that isn't a curve? *(The spec says that if the previous command wasn't a C/S (or a Q/T), the mirror sits on the pen.)*
- **Break it:** stop resetting (`lastC = nextC ?? lastC`). Which test catches it? *("S with no curve before it…")*

### C2 · Arcs in the parser
Point out that `rx`, `ry` and `rotation` are sizes, so they're never made relative. Only the end point is. Flags must be 0 or 1.
- Squashed flags: minifiers write `0110` for "0, 1, 10". The scanner counts arc arguments (`isArcFlag`), and in flag positions reads exactly one character. Ask: what would a plain number regex make of `0110`? *(The number 110, which is the wrong shape, silently.)*
- Error messages carry **character positions** (`scan` returns `positions` alongside `tokens`). Ask why "token 7" would be useless to someone pasting SVG from Figma.

## Part D · Arcs to cubics (the hard maths)

### D1 · Story
An arc instruction says: "draw part of an ellipse this big, from here to there". It doesn't say where the ellipse's centre is, and up to four ellipse pieces could fit. The two flags pick one: big way round or short way (`largeArc`), clockwise or not (`sweep`).

### D2 · Read the five numbered steps in `arcToCubics`
Go through them one at a time; Albert doesn't need to derive the centre maths. It comes straight from the SVG spec (F.6.5), and the comments say so.
- Step 2: ask what should happen if the radius is too small to reach. *(Grow it just enough. Test: "grows a radius that is too small".)*
- Step 5: why cut into quarter turns? *(A single cubic can't draw a perfect circle. It's very close for ≤ 90°, and drifts badly beyond that.)* The magic `k = 4/3·tan(step/4)` is the handle length that best fits a circle piece.
- **Experiment:** in a scratch test, change `Math.PI / 2` to `Math.PI` (half turns). Run "stays on the circle in between" and read how far off it gets.

## Part E · Putting it together: `toCubics`

### E1 · Story
Walk the commands with a pen again, but now every piece comes out as a cubic. Each `M` starts a new subpath (lift the pen). `Z` draws the last edge home (unless already there) and marks the subpath closed.
- Predict: how many segments does `M0 0 L10 0 L10 10 Z` give? *(3: the closing edge counts.)*
- **Break it:** remove the "unless the pen is already there" check. Which test fails, and why does it matter? *(A zero-length segment. Later, when two shapes are matched point by point, that invisible segment steals points and makes the morph pinch.)*
