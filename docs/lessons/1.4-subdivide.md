## A · Why cut at all?

### A1 · Story
Morphing is lerping point to point: point 1 of the star slides to point 1 of the circle, and so on. A square has 4 segments and a circle drawn from arcs has 4, but a star has 10. You can't pair 10 things with 4. So we **cut** the shape with fewer pieces into more pieces, without changing what it looks like, until the counts match.

### A2 · Predict (ask, wait)
- If you snip a piece of string in the middle, does the shape it makes on the table change? *(No. You just have two strings now.)*
- So what has to be true about the two halves of a split curve? *(Together they draw exactly the original curve.)*

## B · `splitCubic`: de Casteljau again

### B1 · Read
It is the same three rounds of `lerpPoint` as `pointAt` (lesson 1.3), but instead of throwing the in-between points away, it **keeps them as the new handles**. The left half is the left edge of the "pyramid" of lerps, and the right half is the right edge.
- Draw the pyramid with him: 4 points → 3 → 2 → 1. Ask: which point do both halves share? *(The top, `mid`, which is the point on the curve at `t`.)*

### B2 · Predict, then check the test
- Split the arch `(0,0) (0,10) (10,10) (10,0)` at 0.5. Where do the halves meet? *(At (5, 7.5), the same as `pointAt(arch, 0.5)` from the 1.3 test.)*

### B3 · Break it
Swap `p2: ab` for `p2: b` in the left half. Predict which tests fail. *("splits in the middle" and "does not change the shape". The halves still meet, but the left one bends differently. A gap-free join is not enough to keep the same shape, and that's why the test samples points along the curve.)*

## C · `subdivide`: matching counts

### C1 · Story
To go from 4 segments to 7, we have to choose which ones to cut. Cut the **longest** first, so the new points spread evenly instead of bunching up.

### C2 · Read the details that make it production-safe
- **Ranking by `hullLength`:** the true length of a curve needs an integral. The control polygon (p0→p1→p2→p3) is cheap and always at least as long, which is good enough for "which is longest".
- **Ties go to the earliest segment.** Ask: why does that matter for a library? *(The same input must give the same output on every machine, or snapshot tests and animations flicker.)*
- **It doesn't modify its input** (`slice()`). Ask: what bug would a user see if it did? *(Their original shape object quietly changes, and the next morph from it starts wrong.)*
- **`RangeError`** for nonsense counts, and never removing segments.

### C3 · Break it
Change `>` to `>=` in the longest-segment loop. Predict which test fails, and explain why. *("breaks ties by taking the earliest segment": ties now go to the last one.)*
